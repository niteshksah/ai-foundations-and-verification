# Week 01 — Learning Journal

## 1. What I Learned

This week, I studied the differences between Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Generative AI, and AI agents. I also learned about prompts, tokens, context, next-token prediction, training, and inference.

I explored how AI can be used for prediction, classification, and generation. I also learned why AI-generated answers should be checked against reliable evidence.

## 2. An AI Answer I Checked

For the hallucination experiment, I asked ChatGPT and Google Gemini for the technical specifications of the Arduino Uno R3.

Both assistants reported the same five main specifications: ATmega328P microcontroller, 5 V operating voltage, 16 MHz clock speed, 32 KB Flash memory, and 2 KB SRAM.

I compared their responses with the official Arduino documentation. I did not find an obvious factual error in those five specifications. The experiment also showed me that source attribution and factual accuracy need to be evaluated separately.

## 3. What AI Did Well

The AI assistants presented the technical specifications in a clear format and explained some of the terminology. Their answers helped me identify which claims needed to be checked against the official documentation.

## 4. What AI Should Not Decide Alone

AI should not be treated as the final authority for decisions involving safety, important technical specifications, or significant consequences. Important claims should be verified, and the responsible person should review decisions that require human judgment or authorization.

## 5. A Question I Still Have

How can I systematically evaluate AI-generated answers when several sources disagree or when no authoritative reference is available?

## 6. What I Will Do Differently Next Week

I will record the exact prompts I use, save genuine evidence of experiments, check important claims against original sources, and clearly distinguish verified results from assumptions or unresolved questions.

## 7. Personal Reflection

This week helped me understand that an answer that sounds convincing is not necessarily correct. I want to develop the habit of checking evidence and documenting my reasoning before relying on AI-generated information.
